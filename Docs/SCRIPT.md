# Red Herring — Script

Companion to `Docs/STORY.md`. Speaker tags are prefixed for a dialogue system.
`[PLAYER]` is the name entered on the main menu. Stage directions in *italics*.
`>>` lines are player choices. `!` marks a line that unlocks a notebook entry.

---

## COLD OPEN — Interrogation Room
*`MainScene`. `Interrogation room-background_0`. Wren sits, one hand open on the table.*

**WREN:** Hands on the table, please. Both of them.

*The player's hands go on the table. `Hands.png` framing.*

**WREN:** Good. That's the last thing I'll ask you to do tonight that isn't a question.
**WREN:** You're [PLAYER]. Nineteen months at the agency. Tonight you worked a private dinner at the Voss estate — arrived seven, board service, off the floor at two.
**WREN:** At one twenty-five this morning, Elias Kerr was stabbed once through the chest on the pantry stair of that house. He was nineteen.
**WREN:** The knife came off your board.

>> "That's not possible."
>> "I want to know who told you that."
>> *(say nothing)*

**WREN:** I've been on since four yesterday afternoon and the Mayor of this city was standing forty feet from that stairwell when it happened. He would like a name before breakfast. Do you understand what that does to a room like this one?
**WREN:** So we're going to do it the slow way, and I'm going to ask you to remember. Not to explain. To *remember*.
**WREN:** Start at eleven.

*Transition to Memory A.*

## MEMORY A — 11:00 PM, The Toast
*`BaronDiningRoom1.png`. Free look. Board in hand.*

**[PLAYER] (V.O.):** The room was full and nobody was hungry. That's the worst kind of night. You just walk.

- **ELIAS:** Table four asked me twice if the cheese was local. It's from a shop on Vell Street. I said the hills.

- **HALLORAN:** *(looks at your board, not at you)* Long night for you people.
- **MIREILLE ANSELM:** Don't apologise. Gaspard plays this one badly on purpose. He knows I hate it.

### The toast — unskippable

**BARON VOSS:** Friends. Sit, sit — no, I'll be brief, you've all heard me be brief before and you know it isn't true.
**BARON VOSS:** Thirty years ago I carried another man's papers for a living. I was not good at it. I was *punctual*, which in that house was the same thing.
**BARON VOSS:** ! To Julien Anselm. Who taught me that a contract is only worth the hand that signs it.
**BARON VOSS:** And to the Mayor, who I am told signs very quickly.

*Laughter. `Hands.png` prompt available: examine hands.*

**[PLAYER] (V.O.):** *(on examining Mireille, twice)* She wasn't listening to the speech. She was looking at his glass.

## ACT I — Interrogation, the knife

**WREN:** Twelve o'clock.
**WREN:** There's a spill. Everybody in that house remembers the spill.

*Transition to Memory B.*

### MEMORY B — 12:00 AM, The Spill
*`BaronDiningRoom2.png`.*

**BARON VOSS:** *(low, not shouting — worse than shouting)* Don't. Move. Don't you dare move, boy.
**ELIAS:** Sir — sir, I have a cloth, I have —
**RUBEN:** Allow me, my lord. *(hands on the Baron's coat)*
**BARON VOSS:** Get *off* —
**RUBEN:** It's setting into the silk. A moment.

*Clatter. Something small hits the tray.*

**[PLAYER] (V.O.):** Something went in the wine on Elias's tray. I thought it was a button.
**[PLAYER] (V.O.):** ! *(examine Baron's hand after)* His hand was bare. There was a white line where the ring had been.
**ELIAS:** *(pocketing his cloth, and something else)* It's fine. It's fine, I've got it.

*Back to interrogation.*

### The Act I questions

**WREN:** Your board. How many knives.
>> "Four. Always four."
**WREN:** There were three on the trolley at two in the morning. The fourth was in Elias Kerr.
**WREN:** So either you handed it to somebody, or you used it.

>> "Or somebody took it."
**WREN:** Off a board you were carrying. Without you noticing.
>> "People take things off a waiter's board all night. That's what a board is."
**WREN:** *(pause)* ...That's the first thing you've said that I'll write down.

**WREN:** One o'clock. Last one. Make it count.

### MEMORY C — 1:00 AM, The Handoff
*`BaronDiningRoom3.png`. The examine-hands verb is the whole scene.*

**[PLAYER] (V.O.):** The party had thinned to the people who wanted something.

- **! Gaspard's hand:** *A napkin round the palm, gone through in two places. When they reach for a key, the fingers don't close. The cuts run across the palm and the inside of the fingers.*
- **! Mireille's hand:** *The pearl bracelet. And a gold band that was not there at eleven.*
- **! Ruben's hands:** *Empty. A small dark case going into his inside pocket.*
- **! Halloran's hands:** *Behind his back. He kept them there the whole time I watched him.*
- **! The Baron's hand:** *Bare. Wine down his front, dried dark.*
- **! Your board:** *Three knives. There are four slots.*

**GASPARD:** *(if approached)* Go and carry something. Please. Go and carry something and don't stand near me.

*Back to interrogation. All five hand entries logged.*

### LOCK 1 — Confront Halloran
*Halloran steps into frame. Right hand in his pocket.*

**HALLORAN:** You've been in here two hours and you've told him nothing.
**WREN:** Chief, I've got it.
**HALLORAN:** You've got a boy with a knife.

>> **ACCUSE:** "Take your hand out of your pocket."

**HALLORAN:** ...Excuse me?
**[PLAYER]:** Gaspard grabbed a blade at half past twelve. You don't take a blade off somebody clean. Whoever was holding it went home cut too.
**[PLAYER]:** You've had that hand in your pocket since you walked in.
**HALLORAN:** *(long silence)*
**HALLORAN:** *(to Wren)* Turn that off.
**WREN:** It isn't on, Chief. You told me not to run it.
**HALLORAN:** *(sits. puts the hand on the table. Four stitches across the base of the thumb.)*
**HALLORAN:** I took the knife. That's it. That's the whole of it. I took it off the board and I never put it back and I have not slept since I saw where it ended up.
**[PLAYER]:** Why.
**HALLORAN:** ! Because he'd lost something and he wanted it found quiet. That's all it ever was. A man loses a thing at his own party, he doesn't call the police, he *has* the police, and the police go and lean on the staff.
**HALLORAN:** I leaned on Kerr. The piano player put a hand in it. I dropped the knife and I did not pick it back up, and if I could go back to any minute of my life it would be that one.
**WREN:** *(quietly)* What did he lose, Chief.
**HALLORAN:** He never said. He said I'd know it when I had it.

**> NOTEBOOK: Someone was searching the house that night. For an object.**

## ACT II — The ring

**WREN:** All right. All right. We're doing this properly now.
**WREN:** The coroner's report came up an hour ago and I hadn't read past the wound.

*`UI-display-Autospy.png` — see Appendix A.*

**WREN:** "Pale banding, right fourth finger, consistent with recent ring wear."
**WREN:** That boy earned forty a week. He didn't own a ring. And he wasn't wearing one when we found him.

>> "He wasn't wearing one at eleven either."
**WREN:** Then he put one on and lost it inside three hours. On the night he died.

### LOCK 2 — Confront the widow
*The widow is brought in, or `Phone` sequence — see Appendix C. She is wearing it.*

**MIREILLE:** They told me a waiter wanted to see me. I assumed it was about a bill.
**[PLAYER]:** Your driver bought a ring off Elias Kerr at a quarter to one.
**MIREILLE:** He did.
**[PLAYER]:** You're not going to deny it.
**MIREILLE:** Why would I? It's mine. *(she puts her hand flat on the table)*
**MIREILLE:** ! You've had it in your hand — no, don't argue, it went past you on a tray and you thought it was a button. Did you look at it? There's a crow on it. Two heads, facing away from each other. It's a stupid crest. Julien's grandfather bought it off a man in Antwerp.
**MIREILLE:** ! That ring was on my husband's finger the night he died. It was not on it the morning after. I have spent fifteen years going to parties I loathe, standing next to men I loathe, looking at their hands.
**MIREILLE:** Tonight I found it. Eleven o'clock. Raised in a toast. *To Julien Anselm.*
**[PLAYER]:** ...The Baron.
**MIREILLE:** Aldric carried Julien's papers for nine years and he was never once late. And then one night there was a robbery, and nothing was taken except a ring.
**WREN:** Madam, if you knew this at eleven o'clock, why didn't —
**MIREILLE:** Because a ring is not a confession, Detective. It is a piece of gold. I needed it in my hand before I said a word to anyone, and by the time it was in my hand a boy was already dead on a staircase and I did not know it.
**MIREILLE:** *(to [PLAYER])* And I am sorry. Because he was dead for the reason you are about to say out loud.

**> NOTEBOOK: The ring is Julien Anselm's. It was on the Baron's hand for fifteen years.**

## ACT III — The name

**WREN:** Say it, then. On the record. It's on now.

>> **FINAL ACCUSATION** — *(the suspect list, `obj-SubjectList.png`, opens. See Appendix B.)*

### If the player names the Baron

**[PLAYER]:** Elias Kerr picked something out of a wine puddle at midnight because he thought it was worth a month's wages. He was right. It was worth a man's whole life.
**[PLAYER]:** Halloran couldn't find it. Ruben already had it. And at twenty past one the Baron came down to the corridor for his knife back and found Elias on the stair.
**WREN:** And a boy who'd sold it. That's a beating. That isn't a killing.
**[PLAYER]:** It's a killing if the boy looked at it first.
**[PLAYER]:** He'd have wanted to know what he was selling. He'd have turned it over and he'd have seen a crow with two heads, and he'd have gone back and asked the one man in that house who'd say a name in front of ninety people.
**[PLAYER]:** *"To Julien Anselm."*
**[PLAYER]:** Elias said that name on the stair. That's what he did wrong. That's all he did.

*`Garage-deathScene.png` — hold.*

**WREN:** *(after a while)* ...His hands were bare in your last memory.
**[PLAYER]:** There was wine down his shirt. Everyone saw wine down his shirt all night. Nobody was going to look at it twice.

### Closing

**HALLORAN:** *(from the door)* You know what happens to me now.
**[PLAYER]:** I know what happens to you either way. You just get to pick which one.
**HALLORAN:** *(long pause. takes out his notebook.)* Wren. Wake the Mayor.

*Wren slides the player's hands off the table — the open-palm gesture from the cold open, reversed.*

**WREN:** You can put them down.

**END.**

### Fail state — if the player names anyone else
**WREN:** *(closing the folder)* That's a name. It's not the right one, and it's not the one they wanted either, so nobody gets what they came for.
**WREN:** Get some sleep. You'll be here again tomorrow.
*(Return to the last unsolved lock.)*

WEAPON     Straight blade, 15cm, narrow. Boning pattern.
           Consistent with catering set, 4-pc.
TIME       0115 - 0135.
NOTE       Body moved approx. 1m post-mortem. Drag,
           not carry. Assailant of comparable weight

HANDS      No defensive wounds. He did not fight.
           Pale banding, right fourth finger. Recent
           ring wear, est. under 6 hrs. No ring
           recovered on the body or at the scene.

*The two entries the player must join: **no defensive wounds** (he wasn't afraid of who he
was talking to — he was bargaining) and **the pale band**.*

# Appendix C — The phone
*`UI-Phone.png` is unused. Suggested job: it is the one call you are allowed.*

Gate each act behind spending it. Act I you can call the agency (alibi — useless, they hang
up). Act II you can call the Voss estate and ask for the widow, which is how she ends up in
the room. Act III the phone rings on its own and it is Gaspard, who has been sitting by a
phone for four hours working up to it:

**GASPARD:** I was going to let them hang you. I want you to know that I got as far as deciding to.
**GASPARD:** I put my hand on it because he was nineteen and I have been in that house since before he was born and I have never once put my hand on anything.
**GASPARD:** It's a boning knife. Four of them. Your board.
**GASPARD:** ...Tell them it was the Chief who was holding it. Don't tell them it was the Chief who used it. He didn't. I wish he had. It would be a smaller thing.

*This is also the cleanest place to hide the Act III hint if playtesters stall.*

# Appendix D — Writing notes

- **The Baron never appears in the interrogation room.** He is a man who is toasted, spilled
  on, and named. Keep him at arm's length; the last time you see him is in a memory, bare-handed.
- **Elias never gets a scene of his own.** Everything you learn about him is second-hand and
  most of it is small: he lied about the cheese, he pocketed a button, he wanted to know what
  he was selling. That is deliberate. He should be a person by the end, assembled out of
  nothing but other people's hands.
- **Wren is not corrupt.** He is tired and he is being fed questions. Every time the player
  gives him something real he writes it down. He is the only character who changes.
- **Nobody confesses to murder.** Halloran confesses to theft. Mireille confesses to wanting.
  The Baron is never in the room to confess at all. The player is the only one who says what
  happened, out loud, in one speech — that is the reward for three acts of not being believed.
